# `func_80048900` reconstruction notes

This routine builds a vehicle movement request. It follows the current waypoint
until the path-target gate succeeds, then optionally acquires another target,
checks the probe and contact results, turns toward it, and requests full speed.
The production source at
[`src/game/80048900.c`](../../src/game/80048900.c) now matches all 508 retail
bytes under the manifest's default GCC 2.8.1 configuration. The assembler emits
512 bytes, with four bytes of zero section padding after the routine.

Recovered behavior:

- Fetch the active waypoint through `func_8004CFB4` and evaluate the path gate
  through `func_8004CFFC`. The latter's null-target and failed-path-test cases
  return one; see its separate reconstruction notes.
- When the gate returns zero, compute the waypoint delta, distance, full-speed
  request, and heading, and clear the request's arrival field.
- Otherwise initialize a stopped, arrived request with the vehicle's current
  heading. Return early on status bits `0x82`, a null acquired target, a zero
  probe result, or a nonzero unsigned-short contact result.
- On successful target checks, compute the target delta, distance, and heading;
  clear the arrival field; set status bit `0x200` if needed; and request full
  speed through the signed 20.12 multiply.

The stack frame is 120 bytes. The movement delta starts at `sp + 24`, the
range probe at `sp + 40`, and the 40-byte contact result at `sp + 56`.
Compile-time assertions cover the vector, vehicle prefix, movement request,
and `0xD0`-byte vehicle-type record used by the indexed reach lookup.

Binding the acquired target to `s2` and the initial goal to `s1` leaves the
vehicle in `s3`. The empty probe-pointer constraint gives that value the
lifetime needed for `s0` while retaining direct stack-relative probe stores.

The conditional status update requires two separate loads of the scale
`4096`. Empty `t1` clobbers on both paths prevent moving those assignments
before the branch. Splitting the updated flags from their store lets the
compiler put the flag calculation in the conditional branch's delay slot and
the store in the following jump's delay slot. The inline multiply's scale
binding preserves `t1` for its input, `t0` for the low word, and `v0` for the
high word. These constraints emit no instructions. All branches, calls, field
accesses, arithmetic, and delay slots are verified against the retail bytes.
